## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who rides escalators in public places. It describes where to stand, how to get on and off safely, and when to choose the lift instead.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Follow the local custom and the signs: stand on one side and leave the other free for walkers, unless signs ask everyone to stand still. | Which side is for standing differs between countries and even cities, and some operators ask everyone to stand because walking causes falls. |
| Hold the handrail. | Escalators can stop suddenly, and the handrail is what keeps you upright when they do. |
| Step on and off deliberately, looking down at the comb plate, and move away from the exit straight away. | Most escalator falls happen at the ends, and people behind you are still being carried forward and can't stop. |
| Keep children holding your hand in the middle of the step, and keep loose clothing, scarves and shoelaces clear of the edges. | Small feet, soft shoes and loose fabric can be caught where the steps meet the sides or the comb plate. |
| Use the lift for prams, wheelchairs and luggage trolleys. | A heavy or wheeled load can roll backwards and knock over the people below you. |

## Context

This pattern suits escalators in transport hubs and shopping centres, where crowds and hurrying people meet.

It does not suit moving walkways, which have their own signs, or narrow single-width escalators where nobody can pass. It also isn't for anyone who feels unsteady on moving steps: if that's you, use the lift or stairs without hesitation.

There's a trade-off between letting people walk past and moving the most people safely. Standing on one side lets those in a hurry get by, which is the custom in many places. But some operators ask everyone to stand on both sides, for safety and capacity. This pattern says follow the local custom and the signs, rather than insisting on one rule everywhere.

## Usage

**Before stepping on:** check which side people are standing on, and look for signs about strollers, luggage or standing. If you have a pram, a large suitcase or a trolley, use the lift instead, as described in [Riding an elevator](../elevator-etiquette/).

**Riding:** stand on the standing side, hold the handrail and keep your bag in front of you or on the step beside your feet, not in the walking lane. If you walk, walk steadily and don't push past people who haven't moved over.

**Getting off:** watch for the end of the steps, step off with a slight lift of the foot, and keep walking. Stop to check your phone or the map only once you're well clear of the exit.

**If something goes wrong:** every escalator has an emergency stop button at the top and bottom. Use it if someone falls or something is caught, and call for help.
