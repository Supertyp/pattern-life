## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who does hand embroidery for long periods. It describes how to set up light, seating and your hoop so you can stitch for hours without strain, and how to keep needles and scissors safe.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Fix the light before you reach for magnification. | Bright, even light on the work often helps more than a magnifier, and it reduces eye strain on its own. |
| Bring the work up to you rather than bending down to it. | Sitting upright with the hoop at chest height, or on a stand, saves your neck and shoulders. |
| Take a short break at least every hour, and look into the distance. | Rest lets your eye muscles relax and stops your hands and wrists from stiffening into one position. |
| Keep your grip on the needle and hoop light. | A tight pinch tires the small muscles of your hand and is a common cause of aching fingers. |
| Park needles in a needle-minder or pincushion, and put sharps away after every session. | A dropped needle is hard to find and dangerous to children and pets, who can step on or swallow it. |

## Context

This pattern suits hand embroidery and cross-stitch, whether you hold the hoop or use a stand or frame.

It does not replace medical advice. If you get numbness, tingling or pain that lasts after you stop, see a doctor or physiotherapist rather than stitching through it.

There's a trade-off between holding the hoop and using a stand. Holding it is portable and lets you turn the work easily, but one hand is always working as a clamp. A stand or table clamp frees both hands and improves posture, but costs money and ties you to one place. This pattern recommends a stand for long sessions, and holding the hoop for short ones or when you travel.

## Usage

**Light and magnification:** work in daylight near a window where you can, and in the evening use a task lamp with a daylight-type LED aimed at the hoop from the side opposite your stitching hand, so your hand doesn't cast a shadow. Avoid a single dim ceiling light. If you still struggle to see fine detail, add magnification: about 1.75 to 2.25 times is a common range for embroidery, and higher power means you have to sit closer.

**Posture:** sit in a supportive chair with your feet flat and your back against the backrest. Raise the hoop to chest height on a cushion, a lap stand or a table clamp rather than holding it in your lap and looking down. The same rules as [Set up your desk to fit you](../desk-ergonomics/) apply: your neck should be close to neutral and your shoulders relaxed. A stand also lets you work with one hand on top and one below, which is faster and easier on the shoulders.

**Breaks and pacing:** stitch in blocks with a set end, like [protected blocks of focus time](../focus-blocks/) at work. At least every hour, put the needle in the minder, stand up, roll your shoulders, stretch your fingers and wrists, and look at something more than 6 metres away for a little while. Spread a big project over many sessions rather than one marathon. Shorter thread lengths also mean less arm movement per stitch (see [Managing floss](../managing-thread/)).

**Safe storage:** keep needles in a needle book or case, and while you stitch, park the needle on a magnetic needle-minder or a pincushion, never in the arm of the sofa or your clothing. Count your needles at the start and end of a session if you use several. Store embroidery scissors in a sheath, and keep your kit in a closed box out of reach of children and pets. Thread is a hazard too: cats and dogs can swallow it, so don't leave loose ends lying around.
