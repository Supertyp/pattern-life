## Summary

This pattern is for anyone doing recreational exercise, from jogging and team sports to gym training. It describes a simple warm-up that prepares your body for the session and a short cool-down to finish.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Start with 5 to 10 minutes of light cardio, such as brisk walking, easy jogging or cycling. | It raises your heart rate and body temperature gradually, so muscles and joints move more freely. |
| Follow with dynamic movements: leg swings, arm circles, lunges, hip openers. | Moving through a range of motion prepares you for activity better than holding still. |
| Make the last part specific to what you're about to do. | A few easy strides before a run, or practice passes before a game, rehearse the exact movements at low intensity. |
| Save longer static stretches for after the session. | Holding stretches for a long time just before explosive efforts can briefly reduce power, and they're more comfortable when you're warm. |
| In strength training, do lighter ramp-up sets before your working weight. | A few sets at increasing weights groove the technique and let you check how the movement feels today. |
| Warm up for longer when it's cold, early or you've been sitting all day. | Your body takes longer to loosen up, and stiffness is more noticeable. |
| Finish with 5 to 10 minutes of easy movement as a cool-down. | It brings your breathing and heart rate down gradually and is a good time for gentle stretching. |

## Context

This pattern suits most recreational training: running, cycling, swimming, racket and team sports, and gym sessions.

It does not suit a gentle walk or easy stroll, which is its own warm-up. If you have a heart condition, are returning after an injury or illness, or are pregnant, get advice from your doctor or a physiotherapist about how to warm up and what intensity is right for you.

There's a trade-off between a thorough warm-up and limited time. If you only have 30 minutes, a long warm-up eats into the session. This pattern says never skip it entirely, but trim it to 5 minutes of easy movement plus one or two specific drills, and start the main session gently.

## Usage

**Before:** dress so you can shed a layer once you're warm, as described in [Dress in layers](../dress-in-layers/). Start easy, at a pace where you could still hold a conversation.

**Ramping up:** add dynamic movements for the joints you'll use most, then a few short efforts that build towards your session pace or weight. You should feel warm and slightly out of breath, not tired.

**After:** slow down gradually rather than stopping dead, then stretch the main muscles you used, holding each stretch gently for around 20 to 30 seconds without bouncing.

A good warm-up doesn't replace sensible training load. See [Increase training load gradually](../gradual-progression/) for how much to add each week. If something hurts during the warm-up, rather than just feeling stiff, stop and get it checked.

## References

Your national sports medicine or exercise science body, a qualified coach or a physiotherapist can give warm-up advice specific to your sport and health.
