## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who uses a knife in the kitchen. It describes how to set up the board, hold the food and handle the knife so the blade only goes where you mean it to.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Put a damp cloth or non-slip mat under the board, and cut a flat side onto round food before you slice it. | A board that can't slide and food that can't roll keep the blade on course. |
| Hold the food with a claw grip: fingertips tucked under, knuckles forward against the side of the blade. | The blade rides against your knuckles, and your fingertips stay out of its path. |
| Keep your knife sharp. | A dull knife needs more force and is more likely to skid off the food and into your hand. |
| Never leave a knife in the sink or under a tea towel. | Someone reaching in can't see it. Wash it and put it away, or leave it in plain view at the back of the bench. |
| Let a falling knife fall. | Grabbing at it is how deep cuts happen. Step back and pick it up once it lands. |

## Context

This pattern suits everyday home cooking with chef's knives, paring knives and utility knives.

It does not suit tasks that need special tools or techniques: mandolines (always use the hand guard or a cut-resistant glove), cutting frozen food, or opening tins and packaging, which call for scissors or the right opener, not a kitchen knife.

There's a trade-off between speed and safety. Fast chopping looks impressive but leaves little margin for error. This pattern asks you to cut at a pace where the claw grip stays in place every stroke. Speed comes on its own with practice.

## Usage

**Setting up:** clear the bench, wet and wring out a cloth, and lay it flat under the board. Have a bowl ready for prepared food and one for scraps so you're not sweeping things around near the blade. [Prepare before you cook](../mise-en-place/) explains how to organise the whole job.

**Cutting:** stabilise the food with a flat side down. Curl your guiding hand into a claw, rest the side of the blade against your knuckles and move the claw back a little after each cut. Keep the knife tip on the board for rocking cuts, and let the blade do the work rather than pushing hard.

**Moving food and knives:** scrape chopped food off the board with the back (spine) of the knife, not the edge, which dulls it and puts your fingers at risk. Carry a knife point down, blade facing back and close to your side, and say "knife behind" in a busy kitchen, so that if you bump into someone or trip, the point goes towards the floor. When you've finished, wipe the blade spine-to-edge and put it away. See [Keeping knives sharp](../knife-sharpening/) for keeping the edge in good shape.

**If you cut yourself:** stop, apply firm pressure with a clean cloth and raise the hand. Seek medical help for deep cuts, cuts that won't stop bleeding after about 10 minutes of pressure, or if you can't move or feel the finger normally.
