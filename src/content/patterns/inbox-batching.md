## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who manages a busy work inbox. It describes how to check email at set times, decide what to do with each message once, and keep the inbox close to empty.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Turn off email notifications and check email at a few set times each day, for example three or four. | Pop-ups pull your attention away even when you don't act on them, and most email can wait a couple of hours. |
| Make a decision on every message as you open it, and delete or archive anything you don't need to act on. | Leaving an email for later means reading it again, often several times, and a clear inbox shows only what still needs your attention. |
| Reply straight away if it takes less than about two minutes. | Tracking a quick task takes longer than just doing it. |
| Move longer tasks to your task list or calendar, then archive the email. | Your inbox isn't a good to-do list, because tasks get buried under new mail. |
| Write short emails with a clear subject line and the request first. | Clear messages get faster and more useful replies, and reduce back-and-forth. |

## Context

This pattern suits office work where email is a main channel but not an emergency line.

It does not suit roles where incoming email must be answered within minutes, such as customer support or some operations work. In those roles, use a shared inbox with agreed response times, or adapt the checks to fit your role.

There's a trade-off between response time and focus. Answering instantly can make you seem responsive, but it fragments your day. This pattern accepts a delay of a few hours for most messages. If your workplace expects faster replies, agree on what's urgent and route it through a phone call or chat instead.

## Usage

**Setting up:** turn off notifications, set up an archive folder and choose a task list. Decide your checking times, for example after your first focus block, after lunch and late afternoon. Use the time between checks for the work described in [Protect blocks of focus time](../focus-blocks/).

**Each batch:** work from the newest or oldest message, whichever you prefer, and make a decision on each one: delete, archive, reply, defer or delegate. When you delegate, forward the message to the right person and tell the sender who's dealing with it, so nobody is left waiting. Stop when the inbox is empty or your batch time is up.

**Reducing volume:** unsubscribe from newsletters you don't read, and ask whether some messages would be better as a short agenda item. See [No agenda, no meeting](../meeting-agenda/) for keeping those meetings short.
