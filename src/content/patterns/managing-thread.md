## Summary

This pattern is for anyone stitching with stranded cotton, also called floss. It describes how to cut, separate, thread and handle it so it stays smooth from the first stitch to the last.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Cut a working length of about 40 to 50 cm, roughly from your fingertips to your elbow. | Longer thread wears thin and fuzzy from being pulled through the fabric again and again, and it tangles more. |
| Pull the strands out one at a time, then put back together the number you need. | Pulling two or three at once bunches the rest into knots, and recombined strands lie flatter and cover better. |
| Let the needle dangle every so often to untwist the thread. | Each stitch adds a little twist, and over-twisted thread kinks, knots and makes stitches look ropey. |
| Move the needle along the thread now and then, and start a fresh length when it looks worn. | The thread wears where it bends at the eye, and fuzzy thread spoils the look of the stitches. |
| Cut the end at an angle with sharp scissors before threading. | A clean, pointed end goes through the eye easily, and a frayed one won't. |

## Context

This pattern suits six-stranded cotton and similar stranded threads in surface embroidery and cross-stitch.

It does not suit pearl (perle) cotton, crewel wool or other twisted threads that are used as they come and shouldn't be split, or metallic threads, which need shorter lengths and extra care.

There's a trade-off between thread length and how often you stop. Longer lengths mean fewer starts and finishes, but more wear and more tangles. Sources differ a little: DMC suggests fingertip to elbow and no more than 50 cm, while Mary Corbet often cuts about 45 cm. This pattern settles on 40 to 50 cm. Use shorter lengths for metallics, silks or a loose-weave fabric that roughens the thread quickly.

## Usage

**Separating strands:** cut your length from the skein, keeping the paper band with the colour number on the skein. Hold the cut floss near the top between your thumb and finger, leaving about 2 cm sticking up. Tap the top to fan the strands, pick out one strand and pull it straight up slowly. The rest will bunch up below your fingers, then fall smooth again. Repeat for each strand you need, lay them side by side and smooth them together. Keep spare strands draped over a card or in a labelled bag to use later.

**Threading:** cut the end cleanly at an angle. Flatten it between your fingers and push the eye onto the thread rather than the thread into the eye. If it still won't go, fold the thread tightly over the needle, pinch the fold flat and push the fold through the eye, or use a needle threader. Leave a short tail of about 8 to 10 cm on the needle side. For an even number of strands you can use the loop start in [Starting and ending threads neatly](../starting-and-ending-threads/).

**While stitching:** every few stitches, hold the hoop up and let the needle hang until it stops spinning. If the thread is twisting one way all the time, give the needle a small turn in the opposite direction as you stitch. If a loop starts to form as you pull a stitch through, stop, put the needle in the loop and pull gently sideways until it opens out, rather than pulling harder. Twist matters most for smooth stitches such as [satin stitch](../satin-stitch/) and wrapped ones such as [French knots](../french-knot/).

**Knowing when to stop:** when the thread looks fuzzy, thin or kinked, or you have about 10 cm left, end it off and start a fresh length. Good light makes wear easier to spot; see [Stitching comfortably](../stitching-comfortably/).
