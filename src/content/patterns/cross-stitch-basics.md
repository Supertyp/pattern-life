## Summary

This pattern is for beginners in counted cross-stitch, where you copy a design from a chart onto fabric by counting squares. It describes how to read the chart, where to start, and how to form the crosses so the finished piece looks even.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Make every top stitch lie in the same direction across the whole piece. | Light reflects evenly off crosses that match, while a mix of directions looks patchy even when you can't say why. |
| Start in the centre of both the chart and the fabric. | The design ends up centred, with an even border of spare fabric for framing. |
| Use two strands of stranded cotton (floss) on 14-count Aida, with a blunt tapestry needle. | Two strands cover the fabric well at this size, and the blunt point slips through the holes without splitting the threads. |
| Work blocks of one colour in rows of half stitches, then come back to complete them. | Rows are quicker and more even than stitching one cross at a time, and use less thread. |
| Add back stitch outlines and French knots only after all the crosses are done. | Details stitched on top of finished crosses stay crisp and aren't buried. |

## Context

This pattern suits counted cross-stitch on Aida, a fabric woven in little blocks with clear holes between them, and the same ideas carry over to evenweave and linen.

It does not suit stamped (printed) cross-stitch, where the crosses are printed on the fabric, or freestyle embroidery, where you choose the stitches yourself (see [Planning a design](../planning-stitch-order/)).

There's a trade-off between working in rows and working one cross at a time. The row method (sometimes called the Danish method) is faster and gives an even look on large areas of one colour. Completing each cross before moving on (the English method) is better for scattered single stitches and on linen, where half stitches can slip. Stitchers also differ on which leg goes on top; it doesn't matter, as long as it's always the same. This pattern uses rows for blocks and single crosses for scattered stitches.

## Usage

**Reading the chart:** each square on the chart is one cross, worked over one block of Aida. Each symbol or colour matches a thread number in the key. Arrows on the edges point to the centre lines, and heavier grid lines every 10 squares make counting easier. Mark off areas with a pencil as you finish them so you don't lose your place.

**Getting started:** fold the fabric in half both ways and mark the centre with a pin or a few large tacking stitches. Hoop it (see [Hooping fabric drum-tight](../hooping-fabric/)). Cut a 40 to 50 cm length of floss, separate two strands (see [Managing floss](../managing-thread/)) and thread a size 24 tapestry needle. Avoid a knot on the back, as it makes a lump under the framed piece; a loop start works neatly with two strands (see [Starting and ending threads neatly](../starting-and-ending-threads/)).

**Forming the crosses:** for a row, bring the needle up in the bottom-left hole of the first block and take it down in the top-right hole. Come up in the bottom-left hole of the next block and repeat along the row, making a line of diagonal half stitches. Then come back: up in the bottom-right hole, down in the top-left, so every top stitch slopes the same way. For a single cross, do both legs before moving on: bottom left to top right, then bottom right to top left. On the back you should see neat vertical stitches. Pull just snug enough for the cross to lie flat without pulling the holes larger.

**Finishing details:** when all the crosses are done, add back stitch outlines with one strand (see [Back stitch for crisp lines](../back-stitch/)) and any [French knots](../french-knot/). Then wash and press the piece (see [Washing, pressing and finishing a piece](../finishing-embroidery/)).
