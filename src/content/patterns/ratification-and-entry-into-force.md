## Summary

This pattern is for officials managing an agreement between signature and entry into force, the date it starts to apply. It describes Australia's domestic treaty process, how it compares with major partners, and how to plan one shared timeline.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Prepare the National Interest Analysis alongside the final text, and table both in Parliament soon after signature. | Parliamentary scrutiny can't start until the treaty is tabled, and the analysis is what members and the public read first. |
| Treat the JSCOT inquiry as real scrutiny: brief the committee, answer submissions and respond to its recommendations. | The committee reports on whether Australia should take binding treaty action, and its concerns often shape the implementing laws. |
| Draft the implementing legislation in parallel, and have it passed before entry into force. | Tariff cuts and origin rules need changes to customs law, and the agreement can't enter into force until both Houses have passed them. |
| Map the partner's approval process early and build one joint timeline. | The slowest side sets the date, and the EU, the US and others have quite different steps. |
| Agree the entry-into-force date with the partner, with enough notice for customs and business. | Exporters, importers and customs systems all need time to prepare for the first day of preferences. |

## Context

This pattern suits trade agreements that need parliamentary scrutiny or new laws on at least one side. It follows [Legal scrub and signing](../legal-scrub-and-signing/).

It does not suit minor treaty actions, such as technical amendments, which Australia scrutinises through a streamlined process.

There's a trade-off between ambition and getting the agreement ratified. An agreement that goes further than domestic opinion supports may stall for years in a parliament. Australia's Parliament can't amend a signed treaty, only the implementing laws. Critics argue that scrutiny comes too late, and supporters reply that consultation during negotiations fills that gap.

## Usage

**The Australian steps:** after signature, the treaty is tabled in both Houses with a National Interest Analysis (NIA), which sets out the obligations, costs, benefits and consultation. The Joint Standing Committee on Treaties (JSCOT) has 15 or 20 joint sitting days to inquire, take submissions and report. DFAT suggests allowing four to six months. Implementing legislation, usually customs and tariff amendments, must then pass both Houses. Finally, the parties exchange diplomatic notes confirming their procedures are complete, and the agreement enters into force on the date it specifies. The Australia–UK FTA was signed on 17 December 2021 and entered into force on 31 May 2023.

**Other systems:** in the EU, the Council approves signature and the European Parliament must give its consent. If an agreement covers areas where member states keep responsibility, it is a "mixed agreement" and each member state must also ratify it, which can take years, although parts can be applied provisionally in the meantime. In the United States, Congress passes implementing legislation. When Trade Promotion Authority applies, this is an up-or-down vote without amendment.

**Choosing the date:** coordinate the entry-into-force date through the exchange of notes. Many schedules cut tariffs on each 1 January, so entry into force late in the year can bring a second cut within weeks. Give customs and business plenty of notice, then hand over to [Implementing trade agreements](../implementing-trade-agreements/).
