## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who works at a desk with a computer. It describes how to adjust your chair, screen, keyboard and lighting to fit your body, and how to build movement into the day.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Adjust your chair first, so your feet are flat on the floor and your knees are at about 90°. | The chair sets everything else, and dangling feet put pressure behind your thighs. Use a footrest if the desk is too high for your feet to reach the floor. |
| Support your lower back with the chair's backrest. | Sitting back into a supported curve reduces strain compared with perching forward. |
| Set the keyboard and mouse so your elbows are at about 90° and close to your body, with shoulders relaxed. | Reaching forward or up tenses your shoulders and neck, and keeping wrists straight reduces strain on them. |
| Put the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. | You look slightly down at the centre of the screen, which keeps your neck in a comfortable position. |
| Place the screen about an arm's length away, directly in front of you. | You can read it without leaning in, and you don't twist your neck to one side. |
| Use a laptop stand with a separate keyboard and mouse for regular laptop work. | A laptop on its own can't have both the screen and the keyboard at the right height. |
| Position the screen side-on to windows, and reduce glare from overhead lights. | Light from behind the screen strains your eyes, and light from behind you reflects off it. |
| Change position and move at least every 30 to 60 minutes. | No posture is good for hours, and short, frequent movement breaks matter more than finding the perfect position. |

## Context

This pattern suits office and home workstations where you sit at a desk for much of the day, including sit–stand desks.

It does not replace a professional assessment if you already have pain, an injury or a condition that affects how you sit. In that case, ask your workplace for an ergonomic assessment or see a physiotherapist. People who wear multifocal glasses may need the screen a little lower than described here.

There's a trade-off between a fixed "correct" setup and comfort. The angles above are starting points, not exact rules. This pattern favours adjusting to a neutral, relaxed position and then changing it often, rather than holding one posture all day.

## Usage

**Setting up:** work from the floor up. Adjust the chair height and backrest, add a footrest if needed, then set the keyboard and mouse, then the screen height and distance. Finally, check lighting and glare.

**During the day:** stand up, stretch or walk at least every 30 to 60 minutes, for example when you finish a block of work in [Protect blocks of focus time](../focus-blocks/). Look away from the screen at something distant every so often to rest your eyes.

**Sit–stand desks:** at standing height, keep the same elbow and screen rules, and alternate between sitting and standing rather than standing all day. If you exercise after work, see [Warm up before you train](../warm-up/).

## References

Safe Work Australia and state workplace health and safety regulators publish guidance on workstation setup. Your employer may also offer workstation assessments, and a physiotherapist or occupational therapist can advise on individual needs.
