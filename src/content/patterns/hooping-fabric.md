## Summary

This pattern is for anyone stitching hand embroidery in a hoop. It describes how to set fabric in a hoop so it stays evenly taut while you work, without stretching it out of shape or leaving marks.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Bind the inner ring with cotton twill tape. | The tape grips the fabric so it slips far less, and it cushions stitches if you hoop over them. |
| Choose a hoop that fits the whole design with a little room to spare, usually 15 to 20 cm for a small piece. | You don't have to move the hoop over finished stitches, and smaller hoops hold tension better than large ones. |
| Tighten the screw first, then ease the fabric taut by pulling gently along the straight grain, a little at a time all round. | Even, on-grain tension gives a drum-tight surface without skewing the weave, which would distort the design. |
| Check the tension each time you sit down, and re-tighten when it softens. | All fabric relaxes as you work, and slack fabric puckers under stitches. |
| Take the fabric out of the hoop, or at least loosen it, at the end of each session. | Fabric left clamped for days can keep permanent creases and dirty rings. |

## Context

This pattern suits surface embroidery and cross-stitch on woven cotton, linen or Aida in a wooden or plastic hoop.

It does not suit large or heavily stitched pieces that need constant, even tension for weeks, such as goldwork or big thread paintings. A slate frame or stretcher bars are better for those. It also doesn't suit velvet or delicate fabrics that crush; frame those or use a scrap of muslin over them.

There's a trade-off in how tight to go. Stitchers who "stab" (push the needle straight down, then straight up in two movements) want the fabric drum-tight, while those who "scoop" (take a bite of fabric in one movement, as many do for stem or chain stitch) need a little give. This pattern chooses drum-tight, because it's better for satin stitch and knots and helps beginners keep stitches even. Ease the screw slightly if you prefer to scoop.

## Usage

**Binding:** wrap the inner ring with 100% cotton twill tape about 1 to 1.5 cm wide. Start on the inside of the ring, wrap at a slight angle so each turn just meets the last, and finish on the inside with a few stitches to hold the end. Polyester tape is slippery, so avoid it. You only need to do this once per hoop.

**Hooping:** lay the inner ring on a table, centre the design over it (see [Transferring a design to fabric](../transferring-designs/)), then loosen the outer ring's screw and press it down over both. Keep the screw at about ten o'clock if you're right-handed, or two o'clock if you're left-handed, so the thread doesn't catch on it. Tighten the screw by hand until the fabric just holds. Then work round the hoop, pulling the fabric gently outwards along the lengthwise and crosswise threads, not on the diagonal, and give the screw another turn. The surface should sound like a drum when you tap it, and the weave should still look square.

**Keeping tension:** tap the fabric before each session and whenever stitches start to look loose. If you need to tighten, do it at the screw rather than by tugging at one side. Stop if the hardware creaks or bends.

**Between sessions:** take the fabric out of the hoop when you finish for the day. If it has creased, steam it gently or press it face down on a towel (see [Washing, pressing and finishing a piece](../finishing-embroidery/)). A hoop stand or propping the hoop on a table edge also takes strain off your hands; see [Stitching comfortably](../stitching-comfortably/).
