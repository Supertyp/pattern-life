## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who serves pasta with a sauce. It describes how to time the two and combine them in the pan, so the sauce coats the pasta and everything reaches the table hot.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Have the sauce ready and warm before the pasta goes into the water. | Pasta won't wait. Once it's drained it starts to stick and cool, so the sauce has to be ready for it, not the other way around. |
| Drain the pasta a minute early and finish it in the pan with the sauce over the heat for a minute or two, tossing as it cooks. | The pasta finishes cooking in the sauce and takes up its flavour, and tossing works the sauce onto every piece instead of leaving it on top. |
| Loosen the sauce with splashes of the reserved pasta water. | The starch in the water thickens and binds the sauce so it turns glossy and clings, rather than turning watery or tight. |
| Use just enough sauce to coat the pasta, with little left in the bottom of the pan. | The pasta stays the main part of the dish, and a well-coated piece tastes of both. |
| Warm the bowls, stir in grated hard cheese off the heat at the end, and serve straight away. | Pasta loses heat quickly in a cold bowl, cheese turns stringy or clumpy if it boils, and a sauced pasta tightens up as it sits. |

## Context

This pattern suits most pan sauces: tomato sauces, olive oil and garlic, butter, cream, pesto loosened with pasta water, and slow-cooked meat or vegetable sauces.

It does not suit baked pasta, cold pasta salads, pasta in broth, or a few dishes where a sauce is traditionally spooned over at the table. Uncooked sauces such as pesto shouldn't go over high heat; toss them with the pasta off the heat instead.

There's a trade-off between control and fuss. Saucing the pasta in the pan means one more step and a big enough pan, while spooning sauce on top lets each person choose how much they get. This pattern prefers the pan because the result is far better, but you can keep some sauce back for anyone who wants extra.

## Usage

**Timing:** start the sauce first. While it cooks, bring the pasta water to the boil (see [Boiling pasta](../boiling-pasta/)). For a quick sauce, do your chopping before you turn on the heat (see [Prepare before you cook](../mise-en-place/)). A slow sauce can be made hours ahead or the day before and gently reheated (see [Slow-simmering a sauce](../simmering-sauces/)).

**Combining:** scoop out a cup of pasta water, then drain the pasta, or lift it straight into the sauce pan with tongs or a spider. Over a medium heat, toss or stir for a minute or two, adding pasta water a splash at a time until the sauce looks glossy and coats the pasta. If the pan is too small, put the pasta back in its pot and add the sauce to it. A knob of butter or a drizzle of olive oil at the end makes it silkier.

**Cheese:** take the pan off the heat before adding finely grated Parmesan, pecorino or another hard cheese, then toss again. Put more on the table so people can add their own (see [Setting the table](../setting-the-table/)).

**Serving:** warm the bowls with hot water from the kettle or in a low oven, and tip it out just before serving. Twirl long pasta into a nest with tongs so each bowl gets an even share of sauce. Then call everyone to the table and eat it while it's hot (see [Eating pasta neatly](../eating-pasta/)).
