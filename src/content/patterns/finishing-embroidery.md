## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who has finished a piece of hand embroidery and wants to display it. It describes how to remove the marking, wash and press the piece without harming the stitches, and finish it in a hoop or a frame.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Check every thread and the fabric for colourfastness before the piece gets wet. | Reds and hand-dyed threads can bleed into the fabric, and the stain is very hard to remove. |
| Remove water-soluble marks with a cold-water rinse before any heat touches the piece. | Heat from an iron or hairdryer can set or "ghost" the marks so they never fully come out. |
| Wash gently by hand in cool water with a little mild, non-biological detergent, and never wring. | Agitation and twisting distort the stitches and the fabric grain. |
| Press face down on a thick towel with a warm iron, lifting and placing rather than sliding. | The towel cushions the stitches so they stay raised, and sliding drags and snags them. |
| Finish the back neatly, with a gathered edge and felt in a hoop, or laced over board in a frame. | A tidy back keeps the front taut and protects the stitches from dust and wear. |

## Context

This pattern suits finished freestyle embroidery and counted cross-stitch on cotton or linen, worked in colourfast stranded cotton (floss), which is the six-strand thread sold in skeins.

It does not suit pieces with silk, wool, metallic or hand-dyed (overdyed) threads, beads or glued parts. Spot clean those with a damp cloth, or don't wash them at all. Garments and table linen need their own care routine.

There's a trade-off with washing. A wash removes marks, hand oils and hoop creases, but every wash risks bleeding or shrinkage. This pattern washes only when there are marks or grime to remove. If the piece is clean and was marked with something that doesn't need water, just press it.

## Usage

**Before you wash:** take the piece out of the hoop. Test colourfastness by dampening a corner of a white cotton cloth and pressing it firmly against each colour on the back for a few seconds. If any colour transfers, don't immerse the piece. If you used a water-soluble pen or stabiliser (see [Transferring a design to fabric](../transferring-designs/)), rinse it under cold running water until the marks or stabiliser have completely gone. Heat-erasable pens need a different approach, so follow their maker's instructions.

**Washing and drying:** fill a clean basin with cool water and a few drops of mild, non-biological detergent. Let the piece soak for a few minutes, then swish it gently and press the water through it with flat hands. Rinse in cold water until the water runs clear. If the water turns coloured, stop washing and rinse in cold water straight away. Roll the piece in a clean towel and press to absorb the water, then lay it flat until it's just damp.

**Pressing:** put a thick, folded towel on the ironing board and lay the piece face down on it. Press the back with the iron on a warm (cotton or wool) setting, lifting and placing it, and work out from the centre to straighten the grain. Use a pressing cloth over delicate areas and avoid pressing raised work such as [French knots](../french-knot/) directly. A light press while the piece is still damp removes hoop creases best. Let it dry flat.

**Finishing:** to display it in a hoop, centre the piece in a clean hoop and tighten it drum-tight (see [Hooping fabric drum-tight](../hooping-fabric/)). Trim the excess fabric to about 3 to 4 cm all round, run a line of running stitch around the edge and pull it up to gather the fabric over the inner ring. Then cut a circle of felt just smaller than the inner ring and stitch it on with small whip stitches. For a frame, lace the fabric over acid-free board with strong thread and use a mat or spacers so the glass doesn't press on the stitches.
