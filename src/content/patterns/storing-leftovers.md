## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who keeps cooked food for later. It describes how to cool, store and reheat leftovers so they stay safe and you know what's in the fridge.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Refrigerate cooked food in shallow containers as soon as it stops steaming, and within 2 hours of cooking. | Bacteria grow fastest between 5 °C and 60 °C, and shallow portions get through that range quickly where a big pot stays warm in the middle. |
| Keep your fridge at 5 °C or below and your freezer at about −18 °C. | Cold slows bacterial growth. A cheap fridge thermometer tells you if the setting is right. |
| Label and date each container, and eat leftovers within 2 to 3 days or freeze them. | Some bacteria still grow slowly in the fridge, and you can't see or smell many of them, so the date does the remembering. |
| Reheat only once, until steaming hot all the way through (at least 75 °C). | Thorough reheating kills bacteria that grew during storage, and each extra round of cooling and reheating gives them another chance. |
| When in doubt, throw it out. | Food that's been left out too long or kept too long isn't worth the risk. |

## Context

This pattern suits cooked meals, soups, stews, rice, pasta, and cooked meat and vegetables.

It does not suit food that has already sat out at room temperature for more than 2 hours (Food Standards Australia New Zealand's 2-hour/4-hour guide says refrigerate or use it within 2 hours, use it within 4, and throw it out after that). Cooked rice needs extra care; see [Rinse, measure and rest rice](../rice-cooker-basics/). Be stricter for anyone who is pregnant, elderly, very young or has a weakened immune system.

There's a trade-off between cooling quickly and protecting the fridge. Putting a large, very hot pot straight into the fridge warms everything else in it. This pattern lets food stop steaming first, then splits it into small containers so it cools quickly without heating the rest of the fridge.

## Usage

**Cooling:** let food stand only until the steam has died down. For large pots, such as a stew from the pressure cooker (see [Natural pressure release](../pressure-cooker-natural-release/)), spoon the food into shallow containers or stand the pot in a sink of cold water and stir. Leave lids off or ajar until cool, then seal.

**Storing:** label and date each container and put it on a shelf, not the door, where the temperature is steadier. Keep raw meat below cooked food. Freeze anything you won't eat within 2 to 3 days, and thaw it in the fridge or microwave, not on the bench.

**Reheating:** heat until steaming hot throughout and bubbling at the edges, stirring halfway in the microwave. Take out and reheat only the portion you'll eat. A thermometer takes the guesswork out; see [Cook by temperature, not time](../cook-by-temperature/). Don't put reheated leftovers back in the fridge.
