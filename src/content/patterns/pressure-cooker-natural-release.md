## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who cooks with a pressure cooker. It describes how and when to let the pressure drop by itself rather than releasing it straight away through the valve.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Let the pressure drop on its own for 10 to 15 minutes before touching the valve. | Food keeps cooking gently as the temperature falls, and meat fibres relax instead of tightening up. |
| Count the release time as part of the recipe's total time. | Most recipes assume natural release, so skipping it leaves food underdone or tough. |
| Use natural release for anything starchy or foamy, such as beans, grains, porridge and stock. | A quick release makes the liquid boil up and spray starchy foam out of the valve. |
| Use quick release only for food that overcooks easily, such as green vegetables, seafood and pasta. | These need cooking to stop the moment the time is up. |
| Turn the valve with a long spoon, and keep your hands and face away from it. | The remaining steam is hot enough to scald. |
| Open the lid only once the float valve has dropped, and tilt it away from you. | The lid lock is a safety feature, and the steam trapped under the lid escapes towards the far side. |

## Context

This pattern suits stews, braises, dried beans, stock, rice and porridge.

It does not suit delicate food where a few extra minutes of cooking ruin the texture, or a meal that has to be on the table straight away.

There's a trade-off between time and texture. A full natural release can take 20 to 30 minutes when the cooker is very full. A partial natural release, waiting 10 minutes and then releasing the rest quickly, gets most of the benefit in less time and works well for rice and grains.

## Usage

**Cooking:** when the cooking time ends, leave the cooker alone. Most electric models switch to "keep warm" by themselves; take a stovetop cooker off the heat.

**Releasing:** after 10 to 15 minutes, turn the valve to venting with a long spoon. If foam or liquid comes out of the valve, close it again and wait a few more minutes.

**Opening:** wait for the float valve to drop, then open the lid tilted away from you.

## References

Your pressure cooker's manual is the best source for its valve positions, maximum fill line and recommended release method for each type of food.
