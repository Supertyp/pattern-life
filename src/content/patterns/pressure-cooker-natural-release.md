## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who cooks with a pressure cooker. It describes how and when to let the pressure drop by itself rather than releasing it straight away through the valve.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Let the pressure drop on its own for 10 to 15 minutes before touching the valve, and count this as part of the recipe's time. | Food keeps cooking gently as the temperature falls, meat fibres relax, and most recipes assume this wait. |
| Always use natural release for starchy, foamy or expanding food such as beans, grains, pasta, porridge and stock. | A quick release makes the liquid boil up and spray starchy foam out of the valve. |
| Use quick release only for food that overcooks easily, such as green vegetables and seafood. | These need cooking to stop the moment the time is up. |
| Turn the valve with a long spoon, and keep your hands and face away from it. | The remaining steam is hot enough to scald. |
| Open the lid only once the float valve has dropped, and tilt it away from you. | The lid lock is a safety feature, and the steam trapped under the lid escapes towards the far side. |

## Context

This pattern suits stews, braises, dried beans, stock, rice and porridge.

It does not suit delicate food where a few extra minutes of cooking ruin the texture, or a meal that has to be on the table straight away.

There's a trade-off between time and texture. A full natural release can take 20 to 30 minutes when the cooker is very full. A partial natural release, waiting 10 minutes and then releasing the rest quickly, gets most of the benefit in less time and works well for rice and grains (see also [Rinse, measure and rest rice](../rice-cooker-basics/)).

## Usage

**Cooking:** when the cooking time ends, leave the cooker alone. Most electric models switch to "keep warm" by themselves; take a stovetop cooker off the heat.

**Releasing:** after 10 to 15 minutes, turn the valve to venting with a long spoon. If foam or liquid comes out of the valve, close it again and wait a few more minutes.

**Opening:** wait for the float valve to drop, then open the lid tilted away from you. For meat, check it's cooked through with a thermometer, as in [Cook by temperature, not time](../cook-by-temperature/), and cool any leftovers promptly following [Cooling and storing leftovers](../storing-leftovers/).
