## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who runs or attends work meetings. It describes how to give each meeting a purpose and agenda, keep it to time, and make sure decisions and actions are recorded.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Write the purpose of the meeting in one sentence, and send it with the agenda at least a day ahead. | If you can't say why you're meeting, email may do instead. A shared agenda lets people prepare and decide whether they need to attend. |
| Invite only the people who are needed for the decisions. | Smaller meetings move faster, and others can read the summary. |
| Put a time limit on each agenda item, and start and end on time. | Timeboxing keeps the meeting focused, and running over disrupts everyone's next commitment. |
| Default to shorter meetings, such as 25 or 50 minutes instead of 30 or 60. | People get a short break before their next meeting, and shorter slots encourage focus. |
| Record decisions and actions, each with an owner and a due date, and send a short summary within a day. | Without a named owner, actions tend not to happen, and everyone, including people who didn't attend, has the same record. |

## Context

This pattern suits team meetings, project meetings, planning sessions and decision-making meetings.

It does not suit informal social catch-ups, one-to-one check-ins that are meant to be open, or brainstorming sessions where a loose structure is the point. Even those benefit from a stated purpose and an end time.

There's a trade-off between structure and flexibility. A strict agenda may cut off a useful tangent. This pattern favours structure, but leaves a few minutes at the end for "anything else", and moves larger new topics to a separate meeting.

## Usage

**Before the meeting:** write the purpose, list agenda items with time limits, and attach any papers. Check whether email would do instead. If you're presenting an item, a quick timed run-through helps, as in [Rehearse your talk to time](../short-talk-rehearsal/). If you're invited to a meeting with no purpose or agenda, it's reasonable to ask for one before accepting, and to decline politely if none comes.

**During the meeting:** start on time rather than waiting for latecomers, which penalises those who arrived on time. Restate the purpose and keep to the timeboxes. Note decisions and actions as you go, and read them back at the end.

**After the meeting:** send the summary, with decisions, actions, owners and due dates. Avoid scheduling meetings across others' protected time, as in [Protect blocks of focus time](../focus-blocks/), and keep follow-up emails brief, as in [Process email in batches](../inbox-batching/).
