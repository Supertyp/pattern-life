## Summary

This pattern is for anyone heading out on a day hike. It describes what to tell someone at home before you leave, so that help arrives in the right place at the right time if you don't come back.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Tell a trusted person your route, start point, any alternative routes or side trips, and your expected return time. | Searchers can start in the right place instead of covering a whole park, even if you change plans on the trail. |
| Agree on a clear "call for help if I'm not back by" time. | It avoids both early panic and a long, dangerous delay. |
| Include your car's make, registration and parking spot, who is in the group, and what emergency gear you carry, such as a personal locator beacon. | A car at the trailhead confirms you're still out, and rescuers can plan the search around your group and gear. |
| Check in with your contact as soon as you're back. | It prevents a false alarm and a search for someone who is already home. |

## Context

This pattern suits day walks in national parks, bushland, mountains and coastal tracks.

It does not need much effort for short, popular walks with good phone coverage, where a quick text message to a friend is enough.

There's a trade-off between detail and effort. A written trip intention form is the most useful for searchers, while a text message is quick enough that people actually send it. Many parks and walking clubs offer online forms that strike a balance. In New South Wales, for example, the national parks service's online form emails your trip summary to up to two emergency contacts.

## Usage

**Before leaving:** write down the route, alternatives, group members and any medical conditions, car details, gear and alarm time, and send it to your contact. Leave a copy at home or in the car. If you carry a personal locator beacon, include its ID number.

**On the trail:** stick to the plan where you can, including the [turnaround time](../turnaround-time/) you set. If you change route and have coverage, send a short update.

**If you're overdue:** a trip plan doesn't start a search by itself. Make sure your contact knows to call the police (000 in Australia) with your plan once the alarm time passes and they can't reach you.

**Afterwards:** check in with your contact as soon as you're back, even if you're running late.
