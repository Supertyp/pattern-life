## Summary

This pattern is for anyone heading out on a day hike. It describes what to tell someone at home before you leave, so that help arrives in the right place at the right time if you don't come back.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Tell a trusted person your route, start point and expected return time. | Searchers can start in the right place instead of covering a whole park. |
| Include any alternative routes or side trips you might take. | People often change plans on the trail, and searchers need to know the options. |
| Agree on a clear "call for help if I'm not back by" time. | It avoids both early panic and a long, dangerous delay. |
| Note your car's location, make and registration. | A car at the trailhead confirms that you're still out on the track. |
| List who is in the group and what emergency gear you carry, such as a personal locator beacon. | Rescuers can plan the search around your group and gear. |
| Check in with your contact as soon as you're back. | It prevents a false alarm and a search for someone who is already home. |

## Context

This pattern suits day walks in national parks, bushland, mountains and coastal tracks.

It does not need much effort for short, popular walks with good phone coverage, where a quick text message to a friend is enough.

There's a trade-off between detail and effort. A written trip intention form is the most useful for searchers, while a text message is quick enough that people actually send it. Many parks and walking clubs offer online forms that strike a balance.

## Usage

**Before leaving:** write down the route, alternatives, group members, car details, gear and alarm time, and send it to your contact. Leave a copy at home or in the car.

**On the trail:** stick to the plan where you can, including the [turnaround time](../turnaround-time/) you set. If you change route and have coverage, send a short update.

**Afterwards:** check in with your contact as soon as you're back, even if you're running late.

## References

Your local national park service or bushwalking club usually has a trip intention form and advice on what to include.
