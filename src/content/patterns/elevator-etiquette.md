## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who uses an elevator (or lift) in a shared building. It describes where to stand, how to get on and off, and the small courtesies that keep a crowded lift moving.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Stand to the side of the doors and let everyone out before you get in. | People leaving can't get past you, and the doors close on a car that hasn't emptied yet. |
| Once inside, move to the back and sides, with your backpack off and bags held low in front of you. | It leaves room near the door for the next person, and a backpack takes up the space of another person. |
| Hold the door with the "door open" button, not your arm, foot or bag. | The button holds the door reliably, while sensors may not detect a hand or strap, and holding it for long delays everyone on other floors. |
| Give priority to people with wheelchairs, prams, walking aids or heavy loads, and take the stairs for one or two floors if you're able. | They often have no alternative to the lift, while you may. |
| In a fire or when the alarm sounds, never use the lift. Use the stairs. | Lifts can stop, open on the fire floor or fill with smoke, which is why buildings post this warning beside the doors. |

## Context

This pattern suits busy shared buildings: offices at nine and five, hotels at checkout, apartment blocks, hospitals and train stations.

It does not suit a private home lift, a goods or service lift with its own operating rules, or an evacuation lift that fire wardens are specifically directing people to use.

There's a trade-off between holding the door for someone and keeping the lift moving. A few seconds for someone hurrying towards it is kind, but holding it for a friend still down the corridor makes everyone wait. This pattern says hold it briefly for people you can see, then let it go.

## Usage

![Floor plan of an elevator seen from above, with the back wall at the top and the doors at the bottom. The back corners, back wall and side walls are good places to stand. The middle, the front corner and the button panel are for when it's busy or you're getting off soon. The door zone inside and the exit path outside should be kept clear, and people waiting on the landing stand beside the doors.](./diagrams/elevator-floor-plan.svg)

**Waiting:** press the call button once, for the direction you want to travel. Stand to the side of the doors rather than in front of them, so people can get out in a straight line.

**Getting in:** let the car empty, then step in and move to the back or into a corner. If you're getting off soon and it's crowded, stand near the door instead, and step out briefly at other floors to let people past.

**Riding:** face the door, keep your bag in front of you and keep conversations and phone calls short and quiet, since nobody in a small space can avoid hearing them. If you end up next to the buttons, press floors for others when they ask. If you meet someone you know, save the longer chat for the lobby, where [Leave room in the circle](../open-circle/) helps others join in.

**Getting out:** say "excuse me" and move steadily. If you're blocking the door, step out to let others leave, then step back in.

For moving stairways in the same building, see [Standing and walking on escalators](../escalator-etiquette/).
