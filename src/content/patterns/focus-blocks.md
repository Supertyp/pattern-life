## Summary

This pattern is for anyone whose work mixes deep, demanding tasks with a steady stream of messages and meetings. It describes how to reserve and protect uninterrupted blocks of time for the work that needs them.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Book focus blocks of 60 to 120 minutes in your calendar, at the time of day your energy is highest. | A visible booking stops others filling the time, and it's long enough to get into hard work. Many people concentrate best in the morning, but use whatever time works for you. |
| Decide the one task for each block before it starts. | You don't spend the first part of the block choosing what to do. |
| Turn off email and chat notifications, and put your phone out of sight on silent. | Each interruption costs more than its own time, because it takes a while to get your concentration back. Even a visible phone draws attention. |
| Take a short break between blocks. | Standing up, moving and resting your eyes helps you keep going through the day. |
| Batch small tasks into their own slot. | Email, admin and quick replies fit well between blocks rather than inside them. |

## Context

This pattern suits writing, analysis, programming, design, planning and any task where losing your train of thought is costly.

It does not suit roles where responsiveness is the job, such as a help desk, reception or being on call. In those roles, a shorter block or a rotation with colleagues may be all that's possible.

There's a trade-off between availability and focus. Being reachable at all times feels helpful, but it leaves little room for work that needs depth. This pattern chooses protected time, while making it clear how people can reach you in an emergency. If your team relies on quick answers, agree on blocks together so cover is shared.

## Usage

**Planning:** at the start of each week, book two or more focus blocks and give each a clear task name. Tell your team when you're usually unavailable and how to reach you if something is urgent.

**During a block:** close email and chat, silence your phone and start on the task straight away. If something unrelated comes to mind, jot it on a list and get back to work.

**Between blocks:** stand up, move and look away from the screen, as in [Set up your desk to fit you](../desk-ergonomics/). Then handle messages in one go, using [Process email in batches](../inbox-batching/).

**Protecting the time:** treat a focus block like a meeting. If someone asks for that slot, offer another. Ask for an agenda before giving up a block, as in [No agenda, no meeting](../meeting-agenda/).
