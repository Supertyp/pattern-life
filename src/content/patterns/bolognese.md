## Summary

This pattern is for home cooks who want a bolognese that tastes like the slow meat sauce of Bologna rather than a weeknight mince. It describes how the general kitchen patterns apply to this one dish, and what's specific to it.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| [Dice](../dicing-vegetables/) one onion, one carrot and one celery stick finely, about 3 to 5 mm, and soften them gently in oil or butter (with a little pancetta if you like) without letting them colour. | This soffritto should melt into a sweet base. Big chunks stay crunchy, and burnt onion turns the pot bitter. |
| [Brown](../browning-meat/) about 500 g of beef mince for four, or a mix of beef and pork, in the pot until it sizzles and browns. | Grey, steamed mince gives a flat, boiled taste. The browned bits on the bottom of the pot are where the flavour comes from. |
| [Simmer](../simmering-sauces/) for 2 to 3 hours at the gentlest bubble, with wine, a modest amount of tomato and some milk. | The sauce should be a meat sauce coloured by tomato, not a tomato sauce with meat in it. Long, slow cooking makes the mince tender and the sauce thick and glossy. |
| [Boil](../boiling-pasta/) tagliatelle, or pappardelle or rigatoni, not spaghetti. | The flat, porous egg ribbons and the tubes hold a chunky meat sauce, while it slides off thin, round strands and pools at the bottom of the bowl. |
| [Bring the pasta and sauce together](../saucing-pasta/) in the pan, then serve Parmigiano Reggiano at the table. | Tossing coats every ribbon, so you don't get a dry tangle with a heap of sauce on top. |

## Context

This pattern suits a slow weekend afternoon, a crowd, or a double batch for the freezer.

It does not suit a 30-minute weeknight dinner. When you're short of time, thaw a portion from last time.

There's a trade-off between tradition and taste at home. Bolognese is a family of recipes, not one recipe. In 1982 the Bologna delegation of the Accademia Italiana della Cucina registered a reference recipe with the Bologna Chamber of Commerce, and it updated that recipe in 2023. The registered version uses no garlic or herbs and only a little tomato. Many households add garlic, a bay leaf or more tomato, and cook it in an hour. The reference shows the character of the dish. Stray from it if you like: you're then making your own family's ragù, and that's fine.

## Usage

**Before you start:** get everything ready first ([Prepare before you cook](../mise-en-place/)): the soffritto diced, the meat out of its packet, the wine opened and the tomato measured. Fine dicing takes a while, so use a sharp knife (see [Keeping knives sharp](../knife-sharpening/)) and a steady board (see [Stable board, claw grip](../safe-cutting/)).

**The sauce:** soften the soffritto, then add the meat and brown it. Pour in about a glass of wine and let it bubble until the smell of alcohol has gone. Add a spoonful of tomato paste, about a cup of passata and some stock, then turn the heat right down. Some cooks, Marcella Hazan among them, add milk before the wine. The registered recipe adds it halfway through. Either way, let it cook in completely. Top up with a splash of stock when the sauce looks dry, and season at the end. Mince must reach 75 °C (see [Cook by temperature, not time](../cook-by-temperature/)), which a long simmer easily does.

**At the table:** [set the table](../setting-the-table/) with warmed pasta bowls, a fork (and a spoon for anyone who wants one) and a bowl of grated cheese to pass around. [Twirl the tagliatelle](../eating-pasta/) a few ribbons at a time rather than cutting them.

**Leftovers:** bolognese tastes even better the next day. Cool it quickly and refrigerate it within 2 hours. It keeps for 2 to 3 days in the fridge and freezes well in portions (see [Cooling and storing leftovers](../storing-leftovers/)).
