## Recurring, not one-off

A pattern describes a problem that many people meet again and again. "How to park on a steep hill" is a pattern; "where to park for one particular concert" is not.

## Tested in practice

Each solution should come from real experience. If a recommendation is only theory, say so.

## Context matters

No solution works everywhere. Every pattern states when it **applies** and, just as importantly, when it does not.

## Safety first

Where a pattern involves heat, blades, heights, traffic or the outdoors, safety recommendations come first and are never optional.

## Short and scannable

Lead with the problem and a summary table of recommendations. Details come after, for readers who want them.

## Linked, not isolated

Patterns refer to related patterns. Together they form a small language for everyday life.
